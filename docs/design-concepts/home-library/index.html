Recommended palette. The orange logo feels like a signature, while cool controls leave room for colorful books and the pink reader sprite.

Reading comes first.

The primary action, a saved book, and a current read appear on the first phone screen. Reader identity becomes a small link; goals support the page further down.

A library you can scan.

A list stays the default. Search, shelf switching, sorting, rating filters, and favorites remain close. Long titles wrap; mobile ratings sit beneath the author.

Feedback has a home.

Desktop Help and the phone account menu lead to feedback. The navigation has an opaque background and five destinations, with space reserved beneath scrollable content.

My recommendation: start with B.

Ink & Paper better matches your cooler-palette preference while keeping ShelfSprite recognizable. Its rail trades some horizontal room for persistent navigation. If Library width matters more, B's palette can pair with A's top navigation; palette and shell are separate choices. Neither direction is approved yet.

These are local, interactive design fixtures. Search, sorting, filters, shelves, favorites, and Help work on sample data. Book actions show handoff previews; no account, database, or AI calls occur. All titles are real, but shelves, ratings, goals, and the reader persona are synthetic. Piranesi uses the existing repository cover. Other jackets are typographic placeholders, including an explicit missing-cover example. This is the “no recommendation preview loaded” Home state; personalized recommendations are not invented.
